Most companies in this market compete on **tokenization**. Rivestor works one layer above: a single marketplace and a single token standard, run by RTCS and non-custodial by design. Many of those companies are potential suppliers to Rivestor rather than rivals.

## Positioning

| | One issuer lists (own assets only) | Anyone lists (open supply) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Everyone invests** | Arrived, Lofty | **Rivestor: the open quadrant** |
| **Institutions only** | Ondo | Centrifuge, Securitize |

<Note>
  Positions are simplified from company sites and documentation as of October 2026 and may be out of date. Check each provider's current materials.
</Note>

## How Rivestor differs

| Player | What they do | How Rivestor differs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Securitize, Ondo | Institutional-grade tokenized funds and treasuries | They keep assets inside their own products. Rivestor is non-custodial and lists across issuers. |
| Centrifuge, Midas, RealT | Issue tokenized credit and real estate | They are supply. They can become Rivestor's catalogue rather than competition. |
| Plume | RWA-focused chain and developer ecosystem | Built for developers. Rivestor builds the consumer marketplace on top. |
| IX Swap | Exchange for trading tokenized assets | Trading only. Rivestor adds issuer onboarding, one token standard and retail distribution. |

## A self-reinforcing network

More assets attract more investors, and more investors attract more issuers. The common standard means a new listing lands on rails that already have investors and a secondary market.
